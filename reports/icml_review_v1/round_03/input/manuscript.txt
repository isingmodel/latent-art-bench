1122   The four-painter exploration used 1,536 named and 384 artist-free outputs, three prompt methods and 16 scenes. Its 24
1123   model–painter–method cells had total variance ratios .206–.376 and grouped generated/reference discrimination accuracy
1124   .940–.983. Pooled named energy medians for Monet, Sisley, Pissarro and Cézanne were 2.068, 1.678, 1.975 and 2.142;
1125   corresponding artist-free medians were 1.732, 1.878, 1.854 and 1.657. Only Sisley had a lower pooled named median.
1126   Matched-size real/real medians were .193, .215, .164 and .199. These post-result summaries describe that exploration, not
1127   a general naming effect.
1128
1129   The later controlled panel selected Monet and Cézanne after exploration. It crossed 24 scenes with three models, two
1130   clauses and three repeats; 142 short-scene outputs supplied two prompt-length comparisons. Reference panels contained
1131   38 Monet and 32 Cézanne works, with water/built/land masses based on maintainer-run LLM visual coding. Named-minus-
1132   free energy changes were −.846, −1.818 for Nano Banana 2, −.625, −.896 for FLUX, and −.234, −.056 for GPT Image
1133   2 (Monet, Cézanne). The ﬁrst four null hypotheses were rejected in the original family of eight. A separate 72-output
1134   FLUX collection repeated both directions (−.695, −.952) with rejection in its own four-endpoint family.
1135   A standalone 96-output GPT Image 2/Cézanne comparison on 24 new scenes reduced energy relative to the generic clause
1136   by 1.195 (p = .00001, ﬁxed α = .025), with named/generic total variance ratio .552. It randomized clauses within 48
1137   two-position blocks, used reference class masses (3, 11, 18)/32, and tested a sharp no-effect null with 99,999 within-block
1138   sign draws and plus-one correction. This tests clause effects, not equality of energy alone. A generic clause therefore did
1139   not reproduce the full proximity gain in that panel, but proximity does not establish correct four-artist contrasts. Likewise,
1140   lower total spread does not establish loss of scene information: controlled FLUX/Monet held-repetition scene retrieval rose
1141   from 44.4% to 59.7% under naming. This measures relative feature distinguishability, not veriﬁed prompt adherence.

1760   M. Retrospective held-scene prompted-name identiﬁcation
1761
1762   M.1. Question, chronology and information budgets
1763   The criterion is the recorded painter name in each generation prompt, among Monet, Sisley, Pissarro and Cézanne. It is not
1764   physical authorship or an expert judgment of resemblance. Baseline recognition and its confusions were already known
1765   when this diagnostic was chosen. Its plan, implementation, constructed tests, embedding archives and original analysis
1766   bindings were then frozen before computing any translated outcomes. Thus the ﬁt excludes held-scene images, but the
1767   choice of question did not originate from an untouched test set. We call this a retrospective out-of-fold diagnostic, not a
1768   conﬁrmatory validation experiment.
1769
1770   The reference baseline uses historical examples only. Common translation additionally uses a balanced pool of generated
1771   named-arm examples, with no correspondence between individual training images and painter labels in its arithmetic.
1772   Selecting that pool and verifying its balance requires experimental metadata. The supervised generated-prototype rule
1773   additionally uses those individual painter assignments. These are different information budgets; the supervised result
1774   describes available generated-domain label structure, not a like-for-like zero-shot improvement or alignment with real-art
1775   features.
1776

2400   The recorded request starts exactly follow the randomized allocation, but randomization occurred within contiguous scene

2402   chronological order: repeat 1 started later in 271 pairs and earlier in 233, with a median within-cell separation of 188.8

2405   A retrospective diagnostic differences the 31-dimensional measurements within each model–scene–arm cell and ﬁts a
2406   common linear drift vector against the signed start-time difference. Each conﬁguration has 84 such pairs. Slopes are trained
2407   on 13 scenes and evaluated on the omitted scene. Relative to predicting zero difference, held-out squared-error gains are

2409   negative gain. This particular common linear drift model adds no predictive value. It neither establishes independent
