# Independent scientific review — methods

## Recommendation

**Overall: 4 — marginally below acceptance.** Soundness: **3/4**; presentation: **3/4**; contribution: **2/4**; confidence: **4/5**.

This is a careful, technically coherent empirical audit. The central computations and the new held-scene diagnostic are sound for their stated finite-data targets. My reservation is the importance and reach of the supported empirical contribution, rather than a requirement for a new algorithm. The paper establishes useful counterexamples to conflating proximity, painter contrasts, and recognition, but the findings remain concentrated in one already-inspected four-painter panel. The retrospective extensions improve the interpretation of that panel without independently establishing the breadth or reliability of the proposed evaluation lesson. This is an independent AI scientific assessment, not an acceptance prediction.

## Summary

The paper compares six requested generation configurations on 14 authored outdoor scenes, six prompt clauses and two repeats, totaling 1,008 images. It measures 31 fixed features against a finite collection of 649 historical reproductions, with scaling fitted on 221 development works. Artist centering separates a shared response from labeled contrasts; a projection coefficient measures aligned amplitude, and a cross-repeat inner product estimates squared contrast error under stable means and zero cross-repeat error covariance. Most scene-averaged named-minus-generic feature change is shared across names. On the same images, CLIP and CSD also assign most own-painter prototype gain to a common component, while giving positive Monet–Sisley alignment where the hand features often do not. A retrospective held-scene mean translation changes reference-prototype classification accuracy, with mixed CLIP outcomes, while supervised generated centroids perform better. A further sensitivity quantifies how assumed repeat covariance changes primary-error point orderings.

## Strengths

1. **Precise estimands and correct separation of quantities (pp. 3–4, Sections 4.1–4.3; pp. 11–15, Appendices B–C).** The distinction between aligned amplitude and total error is well explained. The cross-repeat estimator, amplitude/residual identity, and scene-average/scene-variation decomposition are algebraically consistent. Within-repeat artist dependence from centering is handled correctly. The paper does not interpret negative corrected estimates as physical negative error or treat a low-amplitude response as automatically worse.

2. **A useful control beyond merely removing the artist name (pp. 4–7, Sections 5.2 and 5.6; pp. 23–30, Appendices I and L).** The generic oil-painting arm allows the additional naming response to be measured. Reporting the interaction term avoids incorrectly adding the squared generic and naming effects. The exact prototype-gain decomposition gives a concrete, reusable way to audit a commonly used similarity summary. Related-representation agreement and disagreement are both retained.

3. **Responsible handling of retrospective evidence (pp. 7–8, Section 5.7; pp. 33–42, Appendix M).** The transfer diagnostic excludes both repeats of the held scene, uses an untuned translation, and makes its knowledge of a balanced candidate pool explicit. Harmful decisions, adverse configurations, painter recalls, and the stronger supervised comparator are disclosed. The distinction between the named-minus-generic common effect and the generated/reference domain offset is correct and important.

4. **Strong computational traceability (pp. 25–27, 34 and 43–46).** Frozen plans, image/embedding identities, checkpoint details, tests and complete outputs support close scrutiny. I inspected the central implementations, ran the combined transfer/covariance tests (65 passed), and reproduced both new analyses and reports exactly. All 15 science-evidence hashes checked against the round manifest matched. These are valuable verification results, while appropriately not being presented as statistical or perceptual validation.

## Weaknesses

1. **The broad evaluation contribution is not yet convincingly established by the empirical scope.** **Locations:** pp. 1–2, introduction and Section 3.1; pp. 6–8, Sections 5.6–7; pp. 27 and 33–34, Appendices L.4 and M.1–M.3. **Type: additional evidence, not editing.** Four related painters, 14 outdoor briefs, one clause template and one collection session provide a narrow setting in which a large shared response is plausible. Shared movement can encode legitimate characteristics common to this painter group; its magnitude alone does not show that a similarity score is misleading for a user's evaluation purpose. The paper correctly avoids calling it universally undesirable, but then the remaining empirical lesson is a panel-specific decomposition plus disagreement among known metrics. No independent artist group or untouched prompt/session panel tests whether the principal findings persist when the candidate set changes. The existing development references, source crops and two related encoders are informative sensitivities, but do not supply that missing evidence. I do not regard a human study as mandatory for the explicitly nonperceptual claims; an independent, declared panel testing the same diagnostic would more directly strengthen them.

2. **The recognition extension adds limited evidence beyond familiar domain-dependent classification.** **Locations:** p. 2, Section 2; pp. 7–8, Section 5.7; pp. 33–34, Appendix M. **Type: contribution limitation; prior-work discussion can be improved by editing.** The result that a fixed intercept change affects classification while an additive-invariant statistic stays fixed is expected algebraically; the interesting part is the observed magnitude and sign of the accuracy changes. Those outcomes are retrospective and use the same four classes and images that motivated the diagnostic. The supervised generated-centroid advantage likewise demonstrates real label structure, but is not surprising evidence of historical alignment. [Su et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.18633v1) already evaluate generated-image training/retrieval, prototypical classification, held-out prompts and generator-domain generalization (Sections 4–5 and Figure 7). Section 2 should discuss that overlap, not only their prototype-similarity analysis. The present contribution is the specific common-offset diagnostic and its measured outcomes; it is not a new recognition task or a general adaptation result. This does not invalidate the experiment, but limits the additional novelty supporting acceptance.

3. **The repeat correction remains empirically unqualified for the service sampling process.** **Locations:** p. 4, Section 4.3; p. 15, Appendix C.4; p. 44, Appendix N.5; pp. 45–46, Appendix O. **Type: additional evidence for stronger inferential claims.** Randomization within contiguous scene blocks and two requests per cell cannot identify session-wide state or cross-repeat covariance. The common linear timing check does not address these alternatives, and the simulations explicitly show that dependence can invalidate coverage. Appendix O correctly derives an assumed-correlation sensitivity, including the approximately .251 Nano Banana–FLUX point crossing, but neither estimates a plausible correlation range nor evaluates the dependence sensitivity of the headline scene-averaged shared fractions. Thus the adjusted intervals and “noise-corrected” population interpretation remain conditional assumptions. The manuscript discloses this honestly, so I do not treat it as a hidden statistical error. Nevertheless, the inferential evidence is weaker than the extensive precision of the reported tables may suggest. Independent, interleaved collection blocks would be more informative than further scenarios on these same two repeats.

4. **Public reproduction still stops short of the measurement layer.** **Locations:** p. 8, Reproducibility; p. 27, Appendix L.4; pp. 43–45, Appendix N. **Type: artifact-access work.** The complete source pixels are outside the compact repository and no verified public exact-pixel recovery route is supplied. A reader can replay the retained vectors but cannot independently audit preprocessing, source boundaries, or embedding extraction for the full cohort. This matters for an image-measurement paper whose reference corrections change some comparisons. Local hashes and the source census establish consistency, not external accessibility. A reviewable exact-pixel package or a verified per-file retrieval route, with attribution, would materially improve reproducibility.

5. **The main narrative makes readers carry several distinct geometries and targets at once.** **Locations:** pp. 3–8, Sections 4–6, Tables 1–4; pp. 25–42, Appendices L–M. **Type: editing.** Squared-change share, dot-product gain share, centered error, scalar-calibrated error, and three classification rules all answer different questions. The definitions are mostly clear individually, but the paper would benefit from one compact map of each quantity's reference, baseline, invariances and interpretation. In particular, the fact that Eq. 5's offset is different from Eq. 4's common naming effect should be visible before presenting the transfer result. The long confusion-matrix appendix provides completeness but could be supported by a short synthesis of which painter errors drive the gains.

## Questions for the authors

1. What is the intended transferable empirical conclusion beyond the existence of these metric disagreements on the four-painter panel? Which independent candidate-set or prompt-panel result would falsify that conclusion?
2. Does the common naming response primarily reflect characteristics shared by this related painter group? A matched movement-level control or a second artist group would help distinguish that interpretation from a broadly generic response; the present generic oil-painting arm does not resolve it.
3. Can the currently retained exact pixels and full attribution records be made available through an anonymous review artifact or verified recovery manifest?
4. For the new decision task, can the main text summarize the painter-level changes and the audited-reference exceptions, including CSD GPT Image 1's negative change, alongside the original-target aggregate improvements?

## Prior-work assessment

The use of CSD/CLIP prototypes and content-constrained artist prompts is appropriately credited. [Somepalli et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.01292v1) explicitly form artist prototypes by averaging embeddings and evaluate generated-image dot products, so the decomposition addresses an established evaluation practice. The manuscript also correctly acknowledges the [released CSD checkpoint warning](https://github.com/learn2phoenix/CSD). The main missing emphasis is the extent to which Su et al.'s classification and domain-generalization experiments already contextualize the new generated-prototype control. I found no need to require a new algorithm; the limiting question is the strength of the added empirical knowledge.

## Concrete revision priorities

1. **New evidence, highest priority:** test the central common/labeled decomposition and decision diagnostic on a prospectively fixed second artist/content panel or an existing independently held-out benchmark subset. State a narrow empirical hypothesis before seeing those results. Preserve adverse outcomes. This would address contribution more directly than adding more metrics to the same cohort.
2. **New evidence for inference:** obtain independently interleaved repeat blocks if population-level repeat correction and simultaneous-interval conclusions remain central. Separate session variation from within-session request variation. The current covariance curves are useful limitations analysis, not a substitute.
3. **Artifact work:** provide exact-pixel access or verify complete recovery, together with preprocessing code, source selections and attribution records.
4. **Editing:** organize the main contribution around the common/labeled similarity decomposition and the separate decision-offset diagnostic; add the quantity map; strengthen the direct comparison with Su et al.; make the source-view exceptions easy to find.

No credit in this recommendation is assigned to these unperformed studies or release work.

## Ethics

No specific misconduct, participant risk or other ethical violation is evidenced. The paper discloses AI-assisted source auditing and the absence of human validation. Incomplete attribution/release packaging is an outstanding release task explicitly acknowledged in the manuscript, not evidence of an existing violation.
