## Evidence from the inspected pages

**1. Houses in the Snow.** The [credited Flickr item](https://www.flickr.com/photos/78492580@N02/9610751302) describes a “virtual art museum & art gallery Android application.” It gives a taken/uploaded date in August 2013 but no documented original photographic event, camera, or physical-object ancestry. The [Commons description](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Monet_houses_snow_norway_1895.jpg) repeats the Flickr source and ErgSap attribution; its file history records a Flickr-bot transfer. The exact retained [Denver authority URL](https://www.denverartmuseum.org/en/object/39.2017) returned HTTP 404 and “The requested page could not be found.” A timestamp and an attributed online upload cannot authenticate a photograph of the painting.

**2. Kimbell Weeping Willow.** The [Commons file page](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Claude_Monet_Weeping_Willow.jpg) credits Rlbberlin, but its history records an initial 2006 upload, a 2009 Wmpearl replacement, and the current 2019 Rickdotta version described as “Higher resolution.” No distinct capture history is established for those versions. The directly credited [contributor profile](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/User:Rlbberlin) adds no event provenance. The exact [Kimbell object route](https://www.kimbellart.org/collection-object/weeping-willow) returned HTTP 403 with a JavaScript/cookie challenge; it was not bypassed. This is distinct from the Marmottan work in the older audit.

**3. La Mer à l'Estaque.** The [Flickr item](https://www.flickr.com/photos/ergsap/9630298363/) is another ErgsArt gallery/app upload, with a 2013 taken date and no authenticated original photography. In contrast, [catalogue entry 279](https://www.cezannecatalogue.com/catalogue/entry.php?id=279) credits its principal image to “Peter Schälchli, Zürich” and names the Arp Museum/Sammlung Rau accession. It also identifies Vollard archive photograph 248 and cites a historical Venturi photograph. These are useful new provenance leads, not evidence that the Flickr surrogate arose from a different capture. The [direct photograph-248 archive link](https://www.cezannecatalogue.com/resources/stockbooks.php?type=archives&sb=Vollard&sbno=248) returned a broader archive index and a general explanation of archive-number provenance. No pagination or unrelated-record requests followed. It did not establish the needed two suitable capture events.

The specific photographer/institutional credit and archive identifier in entry 279 satisfied the fixed activation rule for the **Cézanne catalogue-to-institution source family** shared by candidates 4–5. The activation event cites the exact response hash and was appended before candidate 4's first request. Activation was based on new provenance detail, not on a qualified pair or a favorable scientific effect.

**4. Millstone and Cistern.** [Catalogue entry 741](https://www.cezannecatalogue.com/catalogue/entry.php?id=741) credits the Barnes Foundation/Bridgeman Art Library and identifies Vollard photograph 465. Its separately dated Rewald/Loran photographs concern the landscape motif, not photographic recaptures of the physical painting; they therefore do not supply the missing units. The [retained Commons file page](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Paul_Cezanne_Meule_et_citerne_en_sous_bois.jpg) records one 2006 Rlbberlin upload and an own-work assertion, without useful camera/event metadata. Its directly linked [Barnes object 5196 route](https://collection.barnesfoundation.org/objects/5196/details) returned HTTP 301. Following the destination would have exceeded this candidate's cap, so it was not requested.

**5. House and Trees.** [Catalogue entry 611](https://www.cezannecatalogue.com/catalogue/entry.php?id=611) identifies BF89 and Vollard photograph 107, without establishing a separately documented contemporary capture pair. The [Commons description](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Paul_Cezanne_Maison_et_arbes.jpg) records one 2006 Rlbberlin upload and an own-work assertion. The directly linked [Barnes object 4702 route](https://collection.barnesfoundation.org/objects/4702/details) returned HTTP 301 at the third-request cap. Its destination was not requested.
