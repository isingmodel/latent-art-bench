## Executive assessment

This is a strong, carefully bounded empirical paper. Its most useful result is the disagreement between evaluation targets: a fitted transformation can better predict named scene means while moving the resulting cloud farther from the painting references. The fixed-mean centering comparison makes the observed scalar penalty more interpretable, and the variance/retrieval results show why aggregate contraction alone cannot describe conditional organization. The paper now presents this argument coherently without claiming a new conditional-evaluation principle or an internal generation mechanism.

The successful prospective Cézanne/generic comparison adds real evidence. On 24 newly specified scenes, the named clause lowers primary energy from 2.166 to .970, with the precommitted 48-pair sharp-null test yielding the Monte Carlo floor, raw p = .00001. This extends the observed proximity benefit to one actual generic-painting alternative. It does not repair the failed four-arm cohort, explain the earlier OAuth named/free result, or prospectively validate the fixed maps. The manuscript preserves those distinctions, the four-painter exploration and both unresolved palette findings.

The remaining limitations are scientific rather than chiefly editorial: central diagnostics were selected after the retained outcomes were seen; the new successful study tests only one painter/control comparison; and the measurement and service assumptions limit interpretation beyond these digital-feature panels. These do not invalidate the stated finite-panel findings, but they prevent an exceptional general contribution. I found no remaining must-fix claim or layout defect for the current bounded conclusions.

## Concrete strengths

**S1 — The evaluation question now connects the results.** Sections 6–7 do more than list proximity, spread and retrieval statistics. Table 10 evaluates the same fitted maps against two distinct targets; Section 6.4 separates the scalar comparison from the origin-induced mean change. The later translation result is retained even though it complicates a simple naming-success story. These comparisons make the paper useful to readers designing distributional evaluations.

**S2 — The new control is actual additional evidence.** Section 5.7 uses fresh outputs, exact fixed clauses, new scene descriptions, unchanged reference features and a prospective test. This addresses a question that transformed retained coordinates alone could not answer: whether naming outperforms one explicit generic painting clause. Its value comes from the design and observed contrast, not the low p-value alone or the number of generated images.

**S3 — Failed and successful evidence are kept separate in the inferential implementation.** The original four-arm cohort yields neither restored endpoints nor complete-case summaries; the successor has fresh observations in both arms and its own fixed allocation. Appendix A.3 is short enough for the paper while retaining the reason both original endpoints are unavailable. This protects the meaning of the new result. The failure itself supplies no positive scientific validation.

These are substantive boundaries that help a reader judge the contribution.

## Genuine weaknesses and residual limits

**W1 — Prospective evidence still does not cover the central map result.** Sections 6–7 use outcomes already exposed when the diagnostics were chosen. Held-scene fitting protects the particular numerical fit from using the held-out observations, but does not erase outcome exposure in selecting the analysis. The later FLUX collection uses the same scene descriptions, and the failed four-arm validation means no new-scene fixed-map comparison is available. Fifteen joint fold reversals out of 24 are informative heterogeneity, not a stable law across scenes. This is a substantive contribution/generalization limit, not a request to relabel the present result as invalid. A future study would add most value by freezing the existing maps, both E and Q targets, weighting and reporting rules before generating a separate scene panel with repeats sufficient for Q. Either persistence or reversal would be informative. No further retained-view grid can supply that evidence.

**W2 — The generic-clause result is narrow and includes delivery behavior.** Section 5.7 compares one Cézanne clause with one generic phrase in one OAuth collection. Reported medium-quality counts differ, 16 generic versus six named, and native dimensions also differ. These are post-assignment aspects of the delivered outcome, not a reason automatically to invalidate the randomized whole-service contrast. They nevertheless prevent attributing the difference solely to artist semantics. The absent free arm, changed scenes, date and repeat count also prevent a control-type explanation for the contrast with the original OAuth near-zero named/free result. A future crossed F/G/N design in one panel and collection, or a service with an enforceable common delivery contract, would address different parts of this limitation. The current text already states the essential boundary; more caveats would not resolve it.

**W3 — The measurement space remains a consequential part of the finding.** Sections 3, 5.5 and 9 use a fixed, interpretable but correlated 31-coordinate geometry. Texture contributes about 47–49% of reference trace; a single LBP coordinate accounts for 83.9% of the controlled one-pixel-blur texture response. Feature omission and processing sensitivities are valuable, but do not validate digital texture as brushwork, establish capture equivalence, or show that these evaluator rankings transfer to another representation. This limits the reach of the evaluation lesson to the tested geometry. Independent captures of the same works would be a more direct future test of a major unresolved alternative than another small perturbation of the same files. No human-rating study is required to support the stated computational claim.

**W4 — Finite-repeat corrections and inferential assumptions remain empirically unverified.** Section 7.2 and Appendix J require stable zero-mean errors independent across repeats/scenes for B* and Q; the palette model likewise assumes stable responses and independent repeat-block errors. Three repetitions and one later service cohort do not establish those assumptions. Randomized request order, successful software tests and exact replay do not establish them either. The sharp-null tests answer a specific conditional question rather than quantifying population effect uncertainty. This is a real rigor limitation even though negative corrected estimates are retained and claims are appropriately restricted. It would take separately planned repeated collections or a better-attested stochastic service contract to address; a post-hoc p-value on the present diagnostic is not a remedy.

**W5 — Public reproduction starts from retained measurements.** Appendix K now supports meaningful public numerical replay, but pixels and raw response bodies remain outside the packages. A reader can inspect the extractor and reproduce the conclusions from vectors, yet cannot independently rerun every extraction or authenticate delivery/backend claims from the release alone. The distinction is correctly disclosed. An exact numerical replay deserves credit; it is not complete independent experimental reproduction. The clause package's new verification is macOS-based, so the earlier archive's cross-platform result should not be generalized to it without its own run.

These are residual limits, not five undisclosed manuscript defects. W1–W4 would require new scientific evidence to remove. The present review does not request reopening a terminal cohort, another replacement collection, additional paid generation or new analyses chosen to improve a review score.

## Literature positioning and substantive novelty

The targeted primary-source reading supports the revised positioning:

- **Su et al. (2025), Sections 3.2–3.5:** artist-name substitutions at fixed content, held-out prompts and named/free CLIP comparisons with real-artist prototypes already exist. Section 2 now acknowledges this directly. The new paper's distinct question is distributional reference proximity and repeated-scene organization under a fitted moment benchmark, not invention of the name control or unseen-prompt evaluation. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.18633v1).
- **Benny et al. (2021), Section 3 and Theorem 2:** conditional evaluation already separates between/within components and shows how unconditional evaluation can look favorable while conditional behavior is poor. This paper does not establish that general principle anew. Its empirical value is the particular map-ranking disagreement in a painter-prompt setting with repeated scenes. [Primary journal article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11263-020-01424-w).
- **Zhang et al. (2025), Sections 4.2–4.3:** increasing prompt complexity can reduce conditional diversity while improving reference-based measures. Thus the coexistence of contraction and proximity improvement alone is not a new broad finding. Holding scene wording fixed, evaluating generated-only transformations, and testing one actual generic clause provide more specific evidence here. [Primary paper, version 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19557v1).
- **Khayatkhoei and AbdAlmageed (2023), Sections 2–3:** inward/outward support geometry can produce asymmetric neighborhood precision/recall behavior in high dimensions. The manuscript correctly uses this as motivation, not proof that its 31-coordinate coverage results are distorted. The observed reference-ball/energy disagreement is a local empirical result. [Primary ICML paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/khayatkhoei23a/khayatkhoei23a.pdf).

These sources were opened and read beyond search snippets. This was a targeted nearest-literature check, not a systematic review of every paper citing these methods. I found no missing nearest source requiring another related-work expansion. The contribution is a **strong empirical application with a useful evaluation counterexample**, not a landmark technique, general theorem or validated explanation of artist imitation. Under the skill's qualitative labels, it is a moderate contribution with substantive utility in its stated domain.

## Structure, minor improvements and priorities

The current sequence works: four-painter context → actual prompt comparisons, including the narrow prospective result → fitted-map diagnostics → conditional organization → separate palette response → measurement boundaries. Moving retrieval methods to the retrieval subsection helps Section 7 explain its argument in order. Operational history is appropriately concentrated in appendices. I do not recommend another section reorder or expanding the failed collection into a main-text experiment.

One optional precision edit is defensible: in the abstract, replace “Adding scale better predicts named scene means in all six primary cells” with “Adding scale lowers the repeat-corrected scene-mean prediction residual in all six cell averages.” This emphasizes the estimator and averaging underlying the claim. Table 10 and Section 7.2 already explain both, so this is a minor refinement, not a condition for the present scores.

Priorities are:

1. **For this manuscript:** preserve the current C/G versus map-validation distinction, the fold counts and all four painters. No additional retained-data computation is necessary for the claims as written. Avoid presenting the new C/G p-value as confirmation of the central map account.
2. **For a future scientific study:** prospectively test the existing moment-map predictions against both targets on a separately specified panel, rather than adding more post-result transformations. State in advance which directional failures would challenge the present empirical pattern.
3. **For wider interpretation:** isolate delivery/capture sensitivity and compare actual clause controls in one consistent design. These address different limitations and should not be substituted for each other.
4. **For public access:** retain the honest numerical-replay scope; if broader platform reproducibility is claimed for the clause addendum, obtain its own documented platform run first.

No essential author clarification is needed to understand the current estimands or endpoint availability. The major open question is scientific: does the observed E/Q ranking disagreement persist when the diagnostic and new scenes are fixed before outcomes? The current manuscript cannot answer that question, and now says so.
