## Overall assessment

This is now a stronger empirical paper than the preceding manuscript. Its most defensible contribution is a fixed-prompt, finite-reference comparison showing that naming can improve measured proximity while leaving a distributional gap, with heterogeneous relationships between contraction, coverage and scene retrieval. The fresh FLUX collection supplies actual new-output support for both naming directions. The image challenges supply substantive information about what the feature families respond to and where their interpretation fails; the common-square analysis reduces one concrete concern about the original naming contrasts. These are scientific additions, rather than documentation alone.

The work remains materially limited by the chosen representation and reference panels, condition-associated rendering, and the narrow temporal scope. Neither palette collection resolves the additional named-clause interaction. The paper appropriately avoids interpreting that as equivalence, and it does not turn the transformation challenge into perceptual or stylistic validation. Its contribution is a useful empirical extension of existing evaluation ideas, not a new general principle or an identified model mechanism. The current text also claims public availability before that step has occurred; this must be resolved before public circulation. No venue-specific acceptance recommendation is implied.

## Specific strengths

1. **The controlled prompt comparison is more informative than a collection-only comparison.** Sections 5.1–5.2 retain identical scene wording across named/free pairs, use reference-class weights, preserve all energy terms in the paired statistic, and report the entire eight-test family. The third-condition conditioning and sharp-null scope are explicit. This directly connects a prompt intervention to the measured distribution instead of attributing an uncontrolled collection difference to painter names.

2. **The paper reports complementary outcomes without forcing agreement.** The energy decomposition explains why contraction does not mechanically produce lower discrepancy. The NB2/Monet cross-painter preference, the OAuth/Cézanne within-scene increase, the FLUX/Monet retrieval improvement, and the texture-removal reversal all constrain the central interpretation. These are informative observed counterexamples, not merely a list of possible limitations.

3. **The follow-up adds real evidence while retaining the original primary analyses.** The common-square results preserve the complete original pairing and multiplicity family. The new 72 FLUX images and 192 palette images are separately analyzed with a prospectively fixed four-endpoint family. Successful collection does not trigger pooling or retroactive promotion of a sensitivity analysis. The actual FLUX replication matters despite its narrower allocation.

4. **The measurement challenge reports failures of simple feature-family interpretation.** Figure 6 retains cross-family responses, and Section 7.2 reports resampling sensitivity and the 83.9% LBP contribution to squared texture displacement under blur. Reporting these results makes the representation's behavior more inspectable; it prevents the large texture response from being mistaken for broad agreement among twelve independent texture measures.

5. **The reproducibility work covers substantially more than the earlier palette replay.** The adapter preserves the scientific functions and retained inputs, separates numerical checks from source-image verification, and maps result families to replay or recorded metadata. The new post-result LBP share is also represented explicitly in the builder rather than left as an unaudited manuscript number. This is useful existing local functionality, although publication remains a separate gate.

## Genuine weaknesses and residual limitations

### W1. The representation still has limited validity for the artistic interpretation that motivates the paper

The new operations establish response to blur, resampling, chroma contraction and tile boundaries. They do not show that the 31-coordinate geometry ranks or distinguishes painterly resemblance. Texture contributes nearly half of reference trace; one coordinate dominates the blur challenge; and one original primary proximity direction reverses in a non-texture view. Central cropping leaves capture differences and composition differences unresolved, while all NB2/FLUX images remain unchanged by that crop. These facts materially limit what “closer” can mean beyond the chosen computational geometry. The current qualification is appropriate, but qualification does not supply the missing validation. A future extension would need an independently motivated alternative representation or a matched-capture/reference study; perceptual claims would require separate evidence. None is necessary to report the present finite-feature result accurately.

### W2. Replication is narrow, and its result summary currently hides a consequential change in baseline spread

The new collection reuses the same selected artists, references, scene panel and service routing over a short interval. One FLUX output per scene/arm cannot reproduce the within-prompt variation or retrieval analyses. More immediately, Section 7.4 reports only energy differences. The retained primary results show fresh free/reference traces of .737 for Monet and .751 for Cézanne, compared with the above-one original FLUX free/reference traces. Fresh own-named/reference traces are .343 and .553; named/free ratios are .466 and .736. Thus the naming direction recurs while a background distributional property differs. A compact descriptive summary of absolute free/named energies and traces would connect the follow-up to the paper's central distributional question and prevent readers from equating directional replication with wholesale reproduction of the original pattern. Changed sample counts and shared controls preclude attributing that difference to time alone.

### W3. Palette inference remains imprecise and dependent on a small unverified error model

Both collections use six fixed scenes and four repeat blocks. The Welch approximation and scene-stratified variance do not estimate date-level variation, and the new interleaving does not prove independence. The resulting intervals still admit substantive decreases and increases beyond the generic clause. One generic sentence also combines artist-name substitution with other linguistic differences, and delivered geometry/quality varies by condition. Whole-service wording makes the estimand honest, but the experiment does not identify an effect at common rendering settings or distinguish response slope from level/saturation with only two palette levels. The reported unresolved result is valid within these constraints; repeated non-rejection does not remove them.

### W4. Public availability is presently an uncompleted assertion, and pixel-level reproducibility remains unavailable

The abstract, conclusion and availability section use completed-release language. The coordinator explicitly identifies this as anticipatory text and says publication/Ubuntu CI are pending. A successful local rehearsal is valuable evidence of calculation portability within the tested environment, but cannot establish anonymous access or successful execution on the hosted target. This is a release gate, not a request for another image study. After publication, raw artwork/generated pixels and transport bodies will still be absent: an external reader can recompute analyses from supplied vectors, but cannot independently check that those vectors came from the claimed pixels using the package alone.

### W5. The narrative is coherent but heavier than the central empirical claim requires

The 20-page main text presents exploration, controlled naming, several later diagnostics, palette estimation, measurement challenges and temporal replication. Definitions are generally precise, yet repeated recitals of distribution matching and processing limits compete with the substantive result hierarchy. The new temporal table is concise, but is less integrated with the proximity/spread argument than several older descriptive diagnostics. A focused consolidation of repeated interpretation, rather than removal of methods or negative findings, would improve scientific emphasis.

## Literature positioning

The nearest comparisons are now described fairly. [Deliège et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.3390/jimaging11120429) evaluate 300 Midjourney v6 images with three experts, using expert-specified historical ranges rather than the present paper's observed reference panel. The current fixed-scene naming intervention is a meaningful design difference, but comparison of shifts, dispersion and overlap is not new here. [Kim et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2517969123) use contextual representations and context-keyword image-to-image experiments, explicitly filtering artist names and style-period labels. The manuscript correctly contrasts this with its text-only name addition without an original composition as input. [Asperti (2026), version 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25609v1) studies CLIP separation and representation probes; it motivates processing scrutiny but does not validate the present 31 features. The manuscript's “visually small” inversion wording is more defensible than invoking a measured human-perception threshold. [Naeem et al. (2020)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/naeem20a.html) already distinguish evaluation dimensions; the contribution should continue to rest on the controlled empirical findings rather than that general distinction.

This was targeted primary-source reading, not an exhaustive novelty search. I read the retained published primary text for Kim (abstract and generation methods) and Deliège (abstract, prompt design and expert-rating methods); live publisher/PMC retrieval had access/challenge limits earlier in this follow-up. I read the arXiv Asperti methods/inversion/robustness material and revisited relevant sections during this review. Naeem's primary proceedings abstract was checked for the evaluation framing. I did not independently reproduce those papers or claim their results establish the present feature interpretation.
