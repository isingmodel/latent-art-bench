\subsection{Aligned artist responses coexist with substantial recovery error}
All 1,008 outputs yield valid measurements, completing the 14-scene panel for
each model. Every reference-aligned slope has a simultaneous interval above
zero (Table~\ref{tab:specificity-models}). Painter names therefore produce a
response beyond a purely shared shift. Yet response amplitude does not track
recovery error. GPT Image 2 nearly matches the reference amplitude
($\beta=.999$), while its corrected error is $D=1.226$. The decomposition
attributes essentially all of that estimate to other artist-contrast directions
(1.229), with a near-zero amplitude-error term ($-.003$). A strong, correctly
sized component can coexist with substantial misalignment.

\subsection{Shared appearance and off-axis differences explain different effects}
The common component accounts for 82.5--95.7\% of the cross-repeat corrected
global named-versus-free squared feature change. Generic/common direction
cosines range from .693 to .888, showing a substantial shared direction.
The generic shifts are nevertheless smaller than the common named shifts in
every model, with nonzero residuals: a generic clause reproduces part of the
shared response, not all of it. A large shared fraction is compatible with
positive artist-specific slopes because these statistics answer different
questions.

For all four OpenAI configurations, off-axis error dominates the decomposition
(Figure~\ref{fig:specificity-diagnostics}a). This is not simply greater variation
between repeated outputs: the repeat correction removes additive sampling-error
bias under its assumptions, and both GPT Image 2.5 configurations have lower
within-scene repeat spread than GPT Image 2 for every painter. Scene averaging
also conceals some error. Nano Banana 2 has lower global error than FLUX
($.723$ versus $.761$), but higher scene-conditional error ($1.109$ versus $.801$).
Their point ordering reverses when scenes are pooled; the primary paired
difference remains unresolved.

\subsection{Full distributions and sensitivity qualify the model comparison}
All 24 model--painter collections have lower measured total variance than their
references, with trace ratios .248--.877
(Figure~\ref{fig:specificity-diagnostics}b). Naming lowers empirical reference
energy relative to the generic clause in 21 of 24 cells. The exceptions are
Flare for Monet and Pissarro, and Sunburst for Monet. These descriptive gains
are neither uniform nor equivalent to artist-geometry recovery. Sunburst has
the lowest observed named energy for Sisley and C\'ezanne, while FLUX has the
lowest for Monet and Pissarro. In particular, Sunburst's C\'ezanne energy falls
from 2.399 under the generic clause to .745 under naming, despite its larger
four-painter geometry error than FLUX. Reference-only projections illustrate
the narrower, displaced clouds without establishing separation in all dimensions
(Figure~\ref{fig:specificity-distributions}).

FLUX remains the smallest-error point estimate after each of 14 scene omissions.
Common-square measurement, equal weighting of the four metadata-derived
reference-content classes, and their combination retain the same two adjusted
model differences. Class weighting changes the reference target, so absolute
errors across targets are not directly comparable. Feature-family views also
qualify the ranking: Nano Banana 2 has slightly lower texture-only error than
FLUX. Removing texture brings Flare and Sunburst's slopes close to one
($.945$ and $.995$), but their errors remain $1.580$ and $1.604$.
The mismatch therefore persists outside texture coordinates. These sensitivity
analyses neither validate the representation perceptually nor remove
reference-content and capture differences.
