\begin{abstract}
Painter instructions can move generated images closer to a painter's works while
changing the variation within a generated collection. We investigate these changes
using 31 interpretable image features and 70 digital reproductions by Monet and
C\'ezanne. A first study comprises 1,006 images from three generation services,
with shared scene descriptions and repeated, randomized prompt comparisons.
Naming lowers primary energy discrepancy in all six service--painter comparisons;
four reject the conditional randomization null after adjustment. Aggregate
variation decreases consistently, but this does not uniformly imply less
within-scene variation or poorer scene distinguishability. In a held-repetition
diagnostic, FLUX/Monet scene retrieval improves from 44.4\% to 59.7\% despite
substantial between-scene contraction. A second, 192-image experiment crosses
artist-free, generic-painting and two painter-name instructions with muted and
vivid palettes. The generic clause reduces the vivid-minus-muted median-chroma
response by 0.853 development-IQR units relative to the artist-free control in a
secondary comparison. Additional Monet and C\'ezanne effects are $-0.284$ and
$-0.018$, with simultaneous 95\% family intervals $[-0.585,0.016]$ and
$[-0.343,0.308]$; neither primary comparison is resolved. The findings distinguish
distributional proximity, concentration, scene distinguishability and color
response. Contraction alone does not establish poorer scene retrieval or
attenuation beyond generic style language.
They concern the delivered services and selected digital reference panels, rather
than perceptual fidelity or an identified internal model mechanism.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
What does it mean for a generated collection to resemble a painter's work?
Individual images can evoke a familiar palette or motif while covering only a
small part of the variation present across the painter's paintings. Conversely,
a collection can be diverse yet systematically differ from those paintings.
Evaluating resemblance therefore requires more than distance to an average image
or average feature vector. It requires comparing distributions and asking what
kind of variation those distributions contain.

Painter naming offers a direct intervention with which to study this problem.
Given the same description of a scene, adding ``in the style of Claude Monet''
changes the artistic instruction while leaving the written scene description
intact. The resulting images need not change only in style: the instruction may
also affect composition, depicted content or service behavior. Nevertheless,
the comparison identifies how the complete generation system responds to an
explicit painter reference under a common prompt design.

We examine three related questions. Does painter naming move generated feature
distributions closer to paintings by that painter? If the generated distribution
contracts, do different requested scenes become less distinguishable? Finally,
does naming reduce the response to a controlled visual instruction beyond the
effect of generic painting language? The second and third questions test a
possible interpretation of contraction as reduced responsiveness; that
interpretation does not follow from a smaller covariance trace alone.

Two studies address these questions for Monet and C\'ezanne. Study~1 compares
1,006 images from three services using 24 shared scene descriptions and three
repetitions per condition. We measure distributional discrepancy and decompose
variation within and between descriptions, then test scene retrieval with held-out
repetitions. Study~2 uses 192 new images from one service to cross four style
instructions with muted and vivid palettes. Comparing a painter clause with a
generic painting-style clause tests whether the named clause changes the response
beyond this generic clause. Both studies use the same fixed interpretable representation
and a 70-work digital reference panel.

The resulting account separates proximity, variation and responsiveness.
Closer distributions can be narrower without losing scene distinguishability,
and response decreases relative to artist-free prompts also occur under a generic
style clause. These distinctions limit what contraction alone establishes about
the original/generated gap. Our target is the
behavior of the delivered image services in selected painter cases, not the
frequency of a phenomenon across art history or a complete measure of style.

\section{Related work}
Quantitative studies of art have used color statistics, image structure and
information-theoretic measures to characterize variation across painting
collections \citep{kim2014,sigaki2018,lee2020}. More recently,
\citet{kim2026} contrasted autoencoder and CLIP representations and tested
context-keyword image-to-image transformations in a study of art-historical
development. We instead use text-only generation to examine
how a painter-name intervention changes the distribution of interpretable
features relative to a finite set of that painter's works.

Research on generated art has also examined artistic imitation and attribution.
AI-Pastiche assesses generated paintings through human authenticity and
prompt-adherence surveys \citep{asperti2025pastiche}. AI-WikiArt uses captions
of original paintings to condition generation and evaluates attribution with
vision-language models \citep{fu2025aiwikiart}. These approaches address judgments
about individual images and their attribution. Our distribution-level analysis
complements them by distinguishing feature proximity from variation across and
within scene descriptions; it does not replace human evaluation.

The preprint by \citet{asperti2026} reports separation between human and generated
images in CLIP space. Generative-model evaluation also distinguishes fidelity from diversity
and coverage \citep{naeem2020}, while preprocessing can substantially affect
feature-based comparisons \citep{parmar2022}. We apply this distinction to
painter naming and use energy discrepancy \citep{szekely2013} to compare full
empirical distributions without reducing either collection to its centroid.

Compositional generation benchmarks separately evaluate attribute binding and
object relations, including color binding \citep{huang2023t2icompbench}. Our
palette intervention measures the global chroma response to an explicit
instruction. It does not score whether a requested color is assigned to a
particular object. Together with held-repetition scene retrieval, it tests
observable implications of reduced responsiveness without equating them with
semantic adherence or an internal conditioning mechanism.

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Study 1: reference paintings and generation design}
The reference panel contains 38 Monet and 32 C\'ezanne works, represented by
Wikimedia-delivered digital reproductions. The painters were selected after
exploratory work on four painters; those exploratory results are not pooled
into this analysis. Reference identities were screened against prior project
exposure records. The available panel is not a probability sample of either
painter's oeuvre. Selection depended on image availability and landscape
eligibility, with one figure-led work excluded during visual coding.

Three broad content classes organize the comparison: water-dominated scenes,
built scenes and land-dominated scenes. Their reference counts are 21/4/13 for
Monet and 3/11/18 for C\'ezanne. Coding was performed through a maintainer-run LLM
visual assessment, without independent human adjudication; 17 mixed or uncertain
annotations remain flagged. These classes provide coarse content adjustment.
They do not match each generated image to a specific original composition.

The prompt inventory contains eight descriptions per content class. Each is used
for both painters, with three repetitions, giving 72 intended images per
service--painter--condition cell. The artist-free prompt begins ``Create an oil
painting on canvas.'' The named prompt adds ``In the style of [painter].'' Both
then give the identical detailed scene description and request only the painting
area, without a frame, signature, letters or watermark. No original painting is
provided to the generator. A third, short-scene named condition on one service
replaces the detailed description with a short scene description; this changes
content specificity as well as prompt length. An exact example appears in
Appendix~\ref{app:prompt}.

The commercial routes request Google's \texttt{gemini-3.1-flash-image}, termed
Nano Banana 2 (NB2), and Black Forest Labs' \texttt{flux.2-max} (FLUX), through
fixed OpenRouter providers. A local OAuth image service requests
\texttt{gpt-image-2}, termed the OAuth service below. These are requested service
identifiers, not attestations of immutable model weights. NB2 and FLUX return
1024-by-1024 images, in JPEG and PNG respectively. The OAuth service returns
PNGs with variable geometry and reported quality: 424 of 430 images report low
quality and six report medium, all six in artist-free conditions. Its prompt
comparison therefore includes possible changes in delivered quality.

Condition order is randomized independently within each service, painter,
description and repetition group. For the OAuth service, all three conditions
are randomly ordered. Repetitions are distributed across eight assigned
collection batches; the realized collection spans approximately 11.5 hours.
Batches do not represent independent backend sessions. The 1,008 assigned
slots yield 1,006 images: 288 each for NB2 and FLUX, and 430 for OAuth. Two
C\'ezanne/OAuth/short-scene requests are refused and remain missing. One transient
FLUX failure is recovered by an exact-payload technical retry. Successful images
are retained without selecting among alternatives or screening for stylistic
resemblance. Generated content classes refer to the intended prompts; adherence
has not been independently assessed.

\subsection{Feature representation and distribution measures}
Each image is measured in 31 interpretable coordinates: 11 color, eight spatial
and 12 digital-texture features (Appendix~\ref{app:features}). The primary
pipeline applies orientation metadata, converts valid embedded color profiles
to sRGB, assumes sRGB for compatible unprofiled files and preserves aspect ratio
at a 512-pixel short side. Images are opaque and resized with Lanczos filtering,
without upsampling or discretionary cropping. Each coordinate is standardized
by its median and interquartile range (IQR) in a separate development collection
of 221 works from four painters, with equal total weight per development painter.
Neither current references nor generated images fit this transformation.

For reference vectors $X=\{x_i\}$ and generated vectors $Y=\{y_j\}$, let
$a_i,b_j$ be nonnegative weights summing to one within each set. We use the
empirical V-statistic energy discrepancy
\begin{align}
\E(X,Y)={}&2\sum_{i,j}a_i b_j\|x_i-y_j\|_2
-\sum_{i,k}a_i a_k\|x_i-x_k\|_2 \nonumber\\
&-\sum_{j,l}b_j b_l\|y_j-y_l\|_2.
\label{eq:energy}
\end{align}
This statistic incorporates distances within and between the two collections.
Its finite-sample baseline need not be zero for independent samples from the
same population. We interpret lower values as closer empirical distributions
in the specified feature geometry, not as a calibrated degree of artistic
similarity.

References have equal weight, $a_i=1/N_p$. If painter $p$ has $N_{pc}$ reference
works in class $c$ and a generated cell has $n_{pc}$ available images in that
class, a generated image receives $b_j=N_{pc}/(N_p n_{pc})$. Thus both distributions
have the reference panel's class proportions. This weighting reduces differences
in broad content mixture but leaves within-class content differences unresolved.

To measure spread, we use the weighted population-form covariance trace
\begin{equation}
\V(Y)=\sum_j b_j\|y_j-\bar y\|_2^2,
\qquad \bar y=\sum_j b_jy_j.
\label{eq:trace}
\end{equation}
We distinguish the generated/reference ratio $\V(Y)/\V(X)$ from the
named/artist-free ratio $\V(Y_{\mathrm N})/\V(Y_{\mathrm F})$.
A value below one has a different comparison target in these two ratios.
Coordinate-wise squared IQR sums provide a complementary spread summary.

Repeated descriptions allow an exact descriptive decomposition. For description
$b$, let $w_b$ be its total image weight and $\bar y_b$ its weighted mean. Then
\begin{equation}
\V(Y)=
\underbrace{\sum_b\sum_{j\in b}b_j\|y_j-\bar y_b\|_2^2}_{W:\ \text{within descriptions}}
+\underbrace{\sum_b w_b\|\bar y_b-\bar y\|_2^2}_{B:\ \text{between description means}}.
\label{eq:decomposition}
\end{equation}
The between-description term can be further separated into within-class and
between-class components. With three repetitions per description, these are
finite-set summaries, not bias-corrected estimates of population variance
components.
In particular, the between-description term also includes noise in means
estimated from only three images.

\subsection{Study 1: prompt contrasts and inference}
The prespecified inferential family comprises six named-minus-artist-free
energy contrasts and two detailed-minus-short-scene named contrasts on OAuth. A
contrast uses complete pairs sharing the description, repetition and assigned
batch, with the same content weights in both conditions. The paired statistic
retains all terms in Equation~\ref{eq:energy}; it is not a difference of average
distances to a reference mean. Appendix~\ref{app:inference} gives the swap identity.

For each contrast, 99,999 Monte Carlo sign assignments yield a two-sided
randomization $p$-value with the plus-one correction. Holm adjustment controls
the fixed family of eight tests at level .05. The sharp null states that prompt
assignment changes neither availability nor measured features in any assigned
position, under the fixed request and technical-retry policy and no interference.
For a pairwise OAuth comparison, the third condition's position is conditioned
on. The tests concern this finite design and reference panel. They do not test
an oeuvre-wide mean effect, perceptual equivalence or equality of original and
generated population distributions. Treatment-dependent call duration or service
carryover could violate no interference.

The Study~1 prompt inventory, primary metrics and eight-test family were specified
before its evaluation features were measured. The subsequent feature-view,
variance-decomposition, reference-influence, painter-interaction, retrieval and
detection/coverage diagnostics are post-result analyses. They are descriptive and
introduce no additional confirmatory tests. Study~2 has a separately specified
two-contrast family, described below.

\subsection{Measurement and reference diagnostics}
We examine three processing pipelines: the primary 512-pixel pipeline, a
256-pixel version and common JPEG encoding at quality 90 with 4:2:0 subsampling
at 512 pixels. Each uses its own development-only scaler. Four feature views
cross these pipelines: all 31 coordinates; 28 coordinates after dropping three
derived multiscale summaries; 31 coordinates divided by the square root of their
family size; and the 19 color/spatial coordinates with texture removed. The
family-rescaled squared distance sums mean squared differences within each
family; it does not equalize observed family variance or perceptual importance.
This gives 12 views per service--painter comparison.

Reference diagnostics delete one work, holding-collection membership group or
content class at a time. Collection groups use exact recorded sets of holding-
collection IDs and do not identify photographic capture workflows. Work and
collection-group deletions preserve the original content-class masses while
reweighting the remaining works within each class; class deletion renormalizes
the surviving class masses. A separate
analysis refits the scaler after omitting each development painter.

\subsection{Scene distinguishability under contraction}
Using the Study~1 detailed named and artist-free conditions, we classify each
held-out repetition by its nearest scene centroid in the fixed feature space.
For each service, painter and condition, centroids use the other two repetitions
of each scene; the held-out image never enters its own or any competing centroid.
Cycling through the three repetitions yields 72 queries per condition. We evaluate
both all 24 scenes and the eight scenes in the query's broad content class.
The latter comparison removes discrimination attributable solely to broad class.
The scaler remains fixed on development paintings.

Retrieval uses Euclidean distances in all 31 coordinates, color alone, spatial
features alone, texture alone and the 19 non-texture coordinates, under each
processing pipeline. Every scene has equal weight. For this diagnostic, variance
components are also recomputed with equal scene weights; these differ from the
reference-class-weighted components in Equation~\ref{eq:decomposition}. The
comparisons are descriptive. Uniform positive rescaling of a condition leaves
nearest-centroid assignments unchanged; retrieval therefore measures relative
scene distinguishability, not absolute response gain. It also does not establish
whether requested objects are present.

\subsection{Study 2: controlled color responsiveness}
Study~2 crosses six fixed landscape descriptions (two per broad class), four
style arms, two palette instructions and four repetitions, yielding 192 images.
All arms begin with the same oil-painting request. The artist-free arm adds no
style clause; the generic arm adds ``In a traditional landscape-painting style.'';
the named arms add the Monet or C\'ezanne clause. The generic clause is a control
for painting-style language, distinct from Study~1's short-scene named condition.
It is a single alternative clause, not a factorial isolation of artist identity
or a representative sample of generic phrasings.
The palette instruction requests either ``a muted, low-chroma palette throughout
the painting'' or ``a vivid, high-chroma palette throughout the painting.'' Scene
text and the closing instruction are otherwise identical. The free and generic
outputs are shared controls for both painters, giving 48 images per arm.

The 24 scene/repetition blocks were randomly ordered, with all eight arm/palette
requests jointly randomized within each block before generation. The same OAuth
service identifier was used, requesting one opaque 1024-by-1024 PNG at medium quality.
All 192 images were retained and measured under all three processing pipelines.
Actual delivery differed from the requested settings: all outputs were nonsquare
RGB PNGs, and 174 reported low quality and 18 medium despite a medium-quality
request. The medium-quality counts were 10, 4, 3 and 1 in the free, generic, Monet
and C\'ezanne arms. These post-request fields were neither filtered nor adjusted
away; the estimand concerns the complete delivered-service response. Acquisition
accounting for a separate incomplete collection is given in
Appendix~\ref{app:response}.

The prespecified endpoint is median pixel CIELAB chroma in the primary
512-pixel pipeline. Its development-standardized value is
\[
C_{\mathrm{img}}=\operatorname{median}_{\mathrm{pixels}}\sqrt{(a^*)^2+(b^*)^2},
\qquad z=\frac{C_{\mathrm{img}}-11.24253}{7.34764}.
\]
The center and IQR are fixed on the prior development collection.
All cross-processing chroma comparisons use
this same primary scale, rather than refitting or changing units. Let
$d(a)$ be the equal-scene mean vivid-minus-muted response for arm $a$. The two
primary interactions are
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_M=d(M)-d(G),\qquad \kappa_C=d(C)-d(G),
 \label{eq:response}
\end{equation}
where $G$ denotes the generic arm and $M,C$ the named arms. Four repeat-block
contrasts per scene estimate each interaction; the two estimates share the
same generic draws. Template-stratified covariance and Welch--Satterthwaite
$t$ approximations provide Bonferroni simultaneous 95\% family intervals and
Holm-adjusted two-sided tests for the two primary interactions. These are
model-based inferences conditional on six fixed scenes, independent repeat-block
errors and stable within-scene response distributions. Randomized dispatch does
not make a weak zero-interaction test exact. Full formulas appear in
Appendix~\ref{app:response}.

Control responses, named-minus-free and generic-minus-free contrasts use nominal
95\% intervals as secondary summaries. A negative interaction supports a smaller
positive chroma response only with positive control responses and no named
response reversal. An interval including zero remains unresolved. Processing
variants and the remaining coordinates are descriptive. The two primary
interactions were specified before Study~2 generation and remain separate from
Study~1's eight-test family.

To connect responsiveness with original paintings, we compare each generated
chroma distribution with the already exposed reference panel using one-dimensional
Wasserstein distance, empirical quantiles and both directions of central-range
occupancy. Quantiles select the first value whose exact rational cumulative
weight reaches the specified probability. We report empirical and equal-class
reference weights; generated distributions have equal class mass and, for the
combined muted/vivid condition, equal polarity mass. The latter is a designed
mixture, not a natural output distribution. These one-coordinate comparisons are
descriptive and do not recalibrate the reference panel as a validated style target.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Painter naming improves primary feature proximity}
Painter naming lowers primary energy discrepancy in all six service--painter
comparisons (Figure~\ref{fig:primary}). NB2 decreases from 4.188 to 3.342 for
Monet and from 4.704 to 2.886 for C\'ezanne; FLUX decreases from 1.990 to 1.365
and from 1.700 to 0.804, respectively. These four contrasts reject the conditional
sharp null after adjustment across the eight-test family
(Table~\ref{tab:contrasts}). The OAuth differences are smaller, and neither
survives adjustment. The detailed-versus-short-scene comparisons likewise provide
no rejection after adjustment. Non-rejection does not establish equivalence.

The same primary feature space also shows that the named collections have less
aggregate spread than the references. Generated/reference trace ratios for the
six detailed named cells range from .354 to .756, whereas the four NB2 and FLUX
artist-free cells range from 1.069 to 1.535. Thus, reduced spread is not a general
property of all generated images in this experiment. It is associated with the
tested painter-naming conditions. The two short-scene named OAuth cells have still
lower reference-relative ratios, .306 for Monet and .395 for C\'ezanne, although
their energy contrasts do not establish an advantage of detailed prompting.

Energy and spread are distinct summaries but are not statistically independent:
Equation~\ref{eq:energy} includes a within-generated distance term. These results
therefore describe the joint behavior of two measurements, not evidence for a
necessary trade-off between fidelity and diversity.

\subsection{Aggregate contraction has different within-prompt components}
The named/artist-free total-trace ratios range from .331 to .697 in the primary
representation. Decomposition reveals different sources of this contraction
(Figure~\ref{fig:variation}). FLUX reduces both within-description and
between-description variation strongly: the respective ratios are .309 and .337
for Monet, and .489 and .451 for C\'ezanne. For NB2/C\'ezanne, within-description
variation decreases to .786 while between-description variation decreases to
.378. Its aggregate reduction therefore conceals a much larger relative change
between descriptions than between repetitions of a description.

The OAuth/C\'ezanne case shows why the distinction is necessary. Total trace
falls to .651 of its artist-free value even though within-description trace
rises to 1.139; between-description trace falls to .583. A tighter overall cloud
can therefore coexist with more variable repetitions. These observations do not
identify a latent model mechanism or demonstrate fewer perceptually distinct
outputs. They locate the measured variation in the actual repeated-prompt design.

\subsection{Contraction is more consistent than proximity across feature views}
All 72 named/artist-free total-trace ratios across the four feature views and
three pipelines are below one, ranging from .308 to .816. Proximity is less
consistent: 66 of 72 energy changes are negative. Both FLUX painter comparisons
and NB2/C\'ezanne remain negative in all 12 view/pipeline combinations. For
NB2/Monet, the 256-pixel color/spatial-only comparison instead increases energy
by .416. The same no-texture view at 512 pixels gives a named/reference trace
ratio of 1.101. Consequently, robust contraction relative to artist-free outputs
does not imply robust below-reference spread or uniformly improved proximity.

The sensitivity has a substantive measurement basis. Texture coordinates
contribute 49.0\% and 47.1\% of primary reference trace for Monet and C\'ezanne.
Some multiscale coordinates are strongly correlated in development data.
Agreement among such coordinates does not constitute independent evidence of the same
artistic property. The diagnostic views probe this dependence without selecting
a preferred representation from the observed effects.

Deleting individual reference works, holding-collection groups or content classes preserves
the direction of all four NB2/FLUX primary proximity changes. These directions
also survive omission of any one development painter from scaling. Some OAuth
changes cross zero under these perturbations. Deletion stability supports the
paid-service comparisons within the available panel, but does not correct shared
reproduction differences or turn that panel into an oeuvre sample.

\subsection{Contraction need not reduce scene distinguishability}
In the primary 31-feature retrieval diagnostic, naming improves accuracy in two
of six service--painter cells and lowers it in four (Figure~\ref{fig:retrieval}).
FLUX/Monet improves from 44.4\% to 59.7\%, although its equal-scene
between-description trace falls to .336 of the artist-free value. Its retrieval
gain remains positive under all three pipelines, including within-class
candidate restriction. This is a direct counterexample to inferring lost scene
distinguishability from contraction alone.

The changes are heterogeneous. For C\'ezanne, accuracy declines from 66.7\% to
41.7\% on NB2 and from 83.3\% to 69.4\% on OAuth. Both declines persist across
all five feature views and three pipelines in both candidate settings. For
Monet, the OAuth decline is smaller within class (84.7\% to 83.3\%) than across
all 24 scenes (79.2\% to 70.8\%). The NB2 difference is one correct query out of
72. Changes in separability therefore vary across services and painters.

\subsection{Generic painting language also reduces color response}
All four Study~2 arms respond positively to vivid rather than muted instructions
(Figure~\ref{fig:response}). The vivid-minus-muted differences are 3.489, 2.636,
2.351 and 2.618 development-IQR units for the free, generic, Monet and C\'ezanne
arms, respectively; all nominal 95\% response intervals are above zero. The
secondary generic-minus-free contrast is $-0.853$, with nominal interval
$[-1.136,-0.570]$, a 24.5\% reduction relative to the artist-free point estimate.
Named-minus-free contrasts are $-1.137$ for Monet and $-0.871$ for C\'ezanne,
with nominal intervals $[-1.375,-0.900]$ and $[-1.062,-0.679]$.
The named-minus-free contrast alone therefore cannot show that the reduction is
specific to painter naming.

The additional painter-minus-generic interactions are $\hat\kappa_M=-0.284$
(simultaneous interval $[-0.585,0.016]$, Holm $p=.0649$) and
$\hat\kappa_C=-0.018$ ($[-0.343,0.308]$, $p=.8886$). Neither primary comparison
resolves the direction. Monet's six scene-specific point estimates are all
negative, whereas C\'ezanne's have three positive and three negative signs.
Non-rejection does not establish equivalence or rule out smaller interactions.

The Monet estimate is similar at 256 pixels ($-.301$) and after JPEG90
processing ($-.308$). The JPEG interval narrowly excludes zero, with upper
endpoint $-.004$, whereas the primary and 256-pixel intervals have upper
endpoints .016 and .003. This processing-dependent boundary crossing does not
replace the prespecified primary result. C\'ezanne remains unresolved under
both variants. The arm means also separate color level from responsiveness:
relative to generic language, C\'ezanne lowers the muted and vivid levels by
.319 and .337 IQR units, respectively, while leaving their average contrast
nearly unchanged. Two levels cannot identify whether such patterns arise from
a shifted operating range, response gain or saturation.

\subsection{Similar means and broad ranges can mask different chroma distributions}
The named arms' equal-polarity mixtures have means relatively close to the
original reference means, yet assign their mass differently

\section{Discussion}
Across these painter cases, adding a name changes distributional proximity and
the organization of variation. Study~1 shows that a generated collection can
move closer to its reference panel while contracting relative to artist-free
outputs. The decomposition prevents interpreting that contraction as a uniform
reduction in variation between repetitions. The retrieval diagnostic further
shows that even substantial between-scene contraction can coexist with better
scene distinguishability. Distances, variance and distinguishability answer
related but different questions.

The controlled experiment tests whether a painter clause attenuates the color
contrast relative to a generic clause. Generic painting language already produces
a substantial decrease relative to artist-free prompts, and the two
named-minus-generic interactions remain
unresolved. Thus named-versus-artist-free attenuation does not by itself isolate
a painter-specific effect. The observed shifts in color level motivate a more
specific account of how style clauses alter the range of responses to palette
instructions. They do not identify attention competition, a latent style prior
or a training-data mechanism: those are possible explanations requiring further
interventions.

Connecting these results to original paintings also requires separating response
capacity from the probability mass assigned to different outputs. The forced
muted/vivid mixtures can place many reference values within their broad range
without approximating the reference density. Conversely, naming improves the
mixture's chroma distance relative to generic language. These findings do not establish that attenuation prevents approaching
the reference chroma distributions. They do not show that intermediate palettes are unreachable, because
no intermediate instruction was tested.

Several limitations bound the interpretation. The reference panel is selected,
small and uneven across content classes; its digital reproduction workflows and
fine-content matching are unresolved. The representation is interpretable but
not a validated model of style. Texture has substantial influence, and the
NB2/Monet proximity reversal under a no-texture processing variant rules out a
representation-independent claim. Reused images across sensitivity analyses do
not supply independent replications. Study~2 concerns one deployed service and
six fixed scenes, with approximate small-repeat inference; no new-scene or
cross-service interaction is estimated.

Delivery also forms part of the treatment response. In Study~1, square paid
outputs contrast with nonsquare references, making high domain-detection accuracy
ambiguous as evidence about style (Appendix~\ref{app:diagnostics}). The OAuth
conditions in both studies differ in returned geometry and reported quality.
Conditioning on these post-request fields would change the estimand and could
select outputs. We therefore retain them and interpret the complete service
response, without claiming a semantic-clause effect at matched rendering settings.
Human judgments and independent reference validation were not collected, so the
computational findings do not establish perceptual fidelity or artistic value.

A focused extension would sample intermediate palette instructions and multiple
generic style phrasings under a new prospective design. This could distinguish
level shifts from changes in response slope and test dependence on the control
sentence. Replication across new scenes and services would address transfer,
while independently curated reproductions and perceptual evaluation would
address the relation of the measured features to artistic style. These are
separate uncertainties; increasing the present cells alone would not resolve all
of them.

\section{Conclusion}
Painter naming often improves primary feature proximity while reducing aggregate
variation in these generated collections. Contraction alone establishes neither
poorer scene distinguishability nor attenuation beyond generic style language.
Scene retrieval can improve under contraction, generic painting language already
changes color response, and the two named-minus-generic interactions remain
unresolved. Retrieval is invariant to uniform rescaling, so its improvement does
not exclude a reduction in absolute response gain. Distribution-based evaluation should therefore
separate proximity, variation, distinguishability and response to controlled
instructions, while treating the reference panel and delivered service as part
of the measurement context.

\section{Paired randomization statistic}
\label{app:inference}
Let $A_i,B_i$ be a retained pair with common weight $b_i$, and define
$C(v)=\sum_j a_j\|v-x_j\|_2$ and
$T(v)=\sum_i b_i(\|v-A_i\|_2+\|v-B_i\|_2)$. Then
\begin{equation}
 c_i=b_i\{2[C(B_i)-C(A_i)]-[T(B_i)-T(A_i)]\},\qquad
 \sum_i c_i=\E(X,B)-\E(X,A).
\end{equation}
Complementary pair swaps produce $\sum_i s_i c_i$, $s_i\in\{-1,1\}$, exactly,
including the within-generated distances. Independent randomized condition
positions justify the sign assignments under the stated sharp null. Repeated
scene descriptions are not treated as independently sampled scenes for
population inference. With $e$ sign draws at least as extreme in absolute value
as the observed statistic, including ties, $p=(1+e)/100{,}000$. All contrasts
retain 24 distinct descriptions; the two missing short-scene outputs reduce only
the C\'ezanne detailed/short-scene comparison to 70 pairs.

\section{Color experiment: estimation and acquisition}
\label{app:response}
For fixed scene $j=1,\ldots,J$, repetition $r=1,\ldots,R$ and arm $a$, let
$D_{jra}=z_{jra,+}-z_{jra,-}$ and
$K_{jr}=(D_{jrM}-D_{jrG},D_{jrC}-D_{jrG})^\top$, with $J=6$ and $R=4$.
Writing $\bar K_j=R^{-1}\sum_r K_{jr}$ and $S_j$ for their within-scene
sample covariance, the estimates and covariance are
\begin{equation}
 \hat\kappa=\frac1J\sum_j\bar K_j,\qquad
 \widehat{\operatorname{Cov}}(\hat\kappa)=\sum_j\frac{S_j}{J^2R}.
\end{equation}
This retains the off-diagonal covariance from shared generic controls. For
endpoint $p$, let $v_{jp}=S_{j,pp}/(J^2R)$. The approximate degrees of freedom are
\begin{equation}
 \nu_p=\frac{(\sum_j v_{jp})^2}{\sum_j v_{jp}^2/(R-1)}.
\end{equation}
The two simultaneous intervals are
$\hat\kappa_p\pm t_{1-.05/(2\cdot2),\nu_p}\sqrt{\sum_jv_{jp}}$.
Two-sided $t$ $p$-values receive Holm adjustment within this two-endpoint family.
The scenes are fixed; between-scene variation of mean responses is not added as
a random-scene variance component. Estimated scene means retain generation noise. The assumptions do not follow from dispatch randomization alone.

An earlier allocation was stopped after 49 images and one complete HTTP 503
response, leaving 142 assigned slots unstarted. A narrowly specified transport
correction and a replacement collection using the same fixed prompts and order
were designated before successful images were visually inspected or their
scientific features measured. The 192-image replacement is the sole Study~2
inferential cohort. The 49 earlier images were measured after replacement
collection ended and remain ancillary, without pooling or filling missing slots.
Their incomplete assigned grid receives no primary inference. All 192
replacement requests succeeded, with no failures, retries or missing measurements.

\section{Supporting Study 1 distribution diagnostics}
\label{app:diagnostics}
\subsection{Relative painter alignment}
Painter specificity uses equal one-third content-class weights for
both painters. With $G_M,G_C$ denoting collections assigned to Monet and
C\'ezanne, define
\begin{equation}
I=\E(X_M,G_M)+\E(X_C,G_C)-\E(X_M,G_C)-\E(X_C,G_M).
\label{eq:specificity}
\end{equation}
Negative $I$ favors own-painter over cross-painter alignment. The within-reference
and within-generated terms cancel, making this a contrast of relative alignment.
Artist-free prompts are identical across the two collection labels and provide
a descriptive control for their arbitrary painter assignment.

Under equal content-class weights, naming also strengthens relative
own-painter alignment. The interaction $I$ changes from .101 to $-.855$ for
NB2, from $-.048$ to $-1.755$ for FLUX and from $-.044$ to $-1.802$ for OAuth.
Its named-minus-free change is negative in all 36 service/view/pipeline
combinations. This joint contrast does not require each generated collection
individually to be closest to its own painter's references. Separately generated
artist-free collections have identical
prompt payloads across painter labels yet nonzero empirical discrepancies.
These controls illustrate finite-set
and collection variability; they do not provide an equivalence threshold.


\subsection{Projection and domain detection}
For visualization, we fit one common principal-component analysis (PCA) per
painter, assigning half the weight to references and sharing half equally across
the seven generated cells, with content weighting within each. All cells use
that painter's common axes. Full-space linear and radial-basis-function (RBF)
kernel-ridge classifiers use six folds holding out entire generated descriptions
and disjoint original works, without tuning or PCA inputs. This grouping respects
the repeated-prompt structure \citep{roberts2017}. Cross-service evaluation trains
on one service and tests on another using the same held-out description/work
scheme. We report balanced accuracy at a fixed zero decision threshold and mean
within-fold AUC separately; the former also depends on calibration.

The common PCA views show overlapping clouds with changes in location and
concentration (Figure~\ref{fig:pca}). The first two components retain 48.4\% of
weighted variation for Monet and 46.0\% for C\'ezanne, leaving more than half
outside the plots. Visual overlap consequently cannot establish equality of the
full distributions. Conversely, separation in a projection would not establish
a difference in artistic style.

\centering\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{common_pca.pdf}

In the full feature space, within-service RBF balanced accuracy ranges from
.893 to .987 across the 14 original/generated comparisons. However, all 576
NB2/FLUX images are square and all 70 references are nonsquare: a rule using
only square geometry separates these domains perfectly. Capture workflows for
the reference reproductions are also unresolved. High classification accuracy
therefore establishes differences between the recorded image domains, not a
validated stylistic distinction.

Cross-service behavior further limits detector interpretation. At the fixed
threshold, naming lowers balanced accuracy in every directed service/painter
comparison for both kernels. Named-condition ranges are .410--.874 for the
linear kernel and .447--.704 for RBF. This need not mean uniformly weaker
ranking discrimination. For Monet with training on FLUX and testing on OAuth,
linear balanced accuracy falls from .904 to .874 while mean within-fold AUC
rises from .953 to .968. The observed threshold transfer includes calibration
changes; below-chance scores are not evidence that generated images have become
more authentic.


\subsection{Classifier specification}
For $d=31$ standardized coordinates, the fixed kernels are
$K_{\mathrm L}(u,v)=u^\top v/d+1$ and
$K_{\mathrm R}(u,v)=\exp(-\|u-v\|_2^2/d)+1$.
The classifiers minimize weighted squared error with a kernel penalty of .01,
using equal total training weight for original and generated labels and the
reference content proportions within each. Training and evaluation memberships
are fixed. Sparse reference classes mean not every test fold contains all three
classes; pooled balanced accuracy uses the complete held-out set. For cross-service evaluation, AUC is computed within each fold and then
averaged across six folds, rather than pooling margins from differently trained
classifiers. Complete kernels, memberships and scores are included with the
analysis code and numeric tables.

The accompanying diagnostic tables retain the complete cross-service and
reference-coverage sensitivity analyses.
