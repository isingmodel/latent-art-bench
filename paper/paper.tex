\begin{abstract}
An image generator may produce a recognizable painter reference without reproducing
variation across that painter's works. We examine how adding a painter's name
changes the feature distribution of generated paintings, using 70 digital
reproductions by Monet and C\'ezanne and 1,006 images from three generation
services. Shared scene descriptions, repeated generation and randomized prompt
order support within-service comparisons. In a fixed representation of 31 color,
spatial and digital-texture features, naming reduces energy discrepancy from the
reference panel in all six service--painter comparisons. The four Nano Banana 2
and FLUX.2 Max contrasts reject the conditional randomization null after Holm
adjustment across eight tests. Named collections have lower empirical aggregate variation than their matched
artist-free collections in all 72 descriptive comparisons across feature views
and processing pipelines. Decomposition shows that this does not uniformly mean less variation
between repetitions of the same prompt. Proximity changes are less consistent across these views: removing
texture and reducing resolution reverses one of those four primary directions. We conclude that painter naming changes both proximity and the
organization of variation in these generated collections. The results concern
measured digital-image distributions; reference selection, reproduction differences
and absent human validation limit conclusions about artistic style.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
What does it mean for a generated collection to resemble a painter's work?
Individual images can evoke a familiar palette or motif while covering only a
small part of the variation present across the painter's paintings. Conversely,
a collection can be diverse yet systematically differ from those paintings.
Evaluating resemblance therefore requires more than distance to an average image
or average feature vector. It requires comparing distributions and asking what
kind of variation those distributions contain.

Painter naming offers a direct intervention with which to study this problem.
Given the same description of a scene, adding ``in the style of Claude Monet''
changes the artistic instruction while leaving the written scene description
intact. The resulting images need not change only in style: the instruction may
also affect composition, depicted content or service behavior. Nevertheless,
the comparison identifies how the complete generation system responds to an
explicit painter reference under a common prompt design.

We study this intervention for Monet and C\'ezanne with two commercial model
routes and a third image-generation service. The experiment uses 24 shared scene
descriptions, three repetitions per description and a reference panel of 70
paintings. We measure discrepancy from each painter's reference distribution
using pairwise feature distances and measure variation within generated sets
separately. A decomposition distinguishes variation between repetitions of the
same description from variation between descriptions. This distinction matters:
a narrower collection could result from more similar responses to different
scenes, more consistent repetitions, or both.

Our contribution is an empirical account of this prompt intervention and its
measurement limits. The primary comparisons show that closer feature
distributions can accompany reduced aggregate variation. Subsequent diagnostics
locate that variation and test the dependence of the findings on feature choice,
image processing and the reference panel. The study is a comparison of selected
painter cases, rather than an estimate of how often a phenomenon occurs across
art history. It also separates observable image statistics from perceptual
judgments: neither proximity nor concentration alone establishes successful
stylistic imitation or a loss of artistic diversity.

\section{Related work}
Quantitative studies of art have used color statistics, image structure and
information-theoretic measures to characterize variation across painting
collections \citep{kim2014,sigaki2018,lee2020}. More recently,
\citet{kim2026} combined learned formal and contextual representations to study
art-historical development, including a context-conditioned image-to-image
experiment. Our question is different: we use text-only generation to examine
how a painter-name intervention changes the distribution of interpretable
features relative to a finite set of that painter's works.

Research on generated art has also examined artistic imitation and attribution.
AI-Pastiche assesses generated paintings through human authenticity and
prompt-adherence surveys \citep{asperti2025pastiche}. AI-WikiArt uses captions
of original paintings to condition generation and evaluates attribution with
vision-language models \citep{fu2025aiwikiart}. These approaches address judgments
about individual images and their attribution. Our distribution-level analysis
complements them by distinguishing feature proximity from variation across and
within scene descriptions; it does not replace human evaluation.

Separation between human and generated paintings in learned feature space is
already documented and investigated by \citet{asperti2026}. Consequently,
scatter separation or detection accuracy alone is not the contribution here.
More generally, generative-model evaluation distinguishes fidelity from diversity
and coverage \citep{naeem2020}, while preprocessing can substantially affect
feature-based comparisons \citep{parmar2022}. We apply this distinction to
painter naming and use energy discrepancy \citep{szekely2013} to compare full
empirical distributions without reducing either collection to its centroid.

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Reference paintings and generation design}
The reference panel contains 38 Monet and 32 C\'ezanne works, represented by
Wikimedia-delivered digital reproductions. The painters were selected after
exploratory work on four painters; those exploratory results are not pooled
into this analysis. Reference identities were screened against prior project
exposure records. The available panel is not a probability sample of either
painter's oeuvre. Selection depended on image availability and landscape
eligibility, with one figure-led work excluded during visual coding.

Three broad content classes organize the comparison: water-dominated scenes,
built scenes and land-dominated scenes. Their reference counts are 21/4/13 for
Monet and 3/11/18 for C\'ezanne. Coding was performed through a maintainer-run LLM
visual assessment, without independent human adjudication; 17 mixed or uncertain
annotations remain flagged. These classes provide coarse content adjustment.
They do not match each generated image to a specific original composition.

The prompt inventory contains eight descriptions per content class. Each is used
for both painters, with three repetitions, giving 72 intended images per
service--painter--condition cell. The artist-free prompt begins ``Create an oil
painting on canvas.'' The named prompt adds ``In the style of [painter].'' Both
then give the identical detailed scene description and request only the painting
area, without a frame, signature, letters or watermark. No original painting is
provided to the generator. A third, generic named condition on one service
replaces the detailed description with a short scene description; this changes
content specificity as well as prompt length. An exact example appears in
Appendix~\ref{app:prompt}.

The commercial routes request Google's \texttt{gemini-3.1-flash-image}, termed
Nano Banana 2 (NB2), and Black Forest Labs' \texttt{flux.2-max} (FLUX), through
fixed OpenRouter providers. A local OAuth image service requests
\texttt{gpt-image-2}, termed the OAuth service below. These are requested service
identifiers, not attestations of immutable model weights. NB2 and FLUX return
1024-by-1024 images, in JPEG and PNG respectively. The OAuth service returns
PNGs with variable geometry and reported quality: 424 of 430 images report low
quality and six report medium, all six in artist-free conditions. Its prompt
comparison therefore includes possible changes in delivered quality.

Condition order is randomized independently within each service, painter,
description and repetition group. For the OAuth service, all three conditions
are randomly ordered. Repetitions are distributed across eight assigned
collection batches; the realized collection spans approximately 11.5 hours.
Batches do not represent independent backend sessions. The 1,008 assigned
slots yield 1,006 images: 288 each for NB2 and FLUX, and 430 for OAuth. Two
C\'ezanne/OAuth/generic requests are refused and remain missing. One transient
FLUX failure is recovered by an exact-payload technical retry. Successful images
are retained without selecting among alternatives or screening for stylistic
resemblance. Generated content classes refer to the intended prompts; adherence
has not been independently assessed.

\subsection{Feature representation and distribution measures}
Each image is measured in 31 interpretable coordinates: 11 color, eight spatial
and 12 digital-texture features (Appendix~\ref{app:features}). The primary
pipeline applies orientation metadata, converts valid embedded color profiles
to sRGB, assumes sRGB for compatible unprofiled files and preserves aspect ratio
at a 512-pixel short side. Images are opaque and resized with Lanczos filtering,
without upsampling or discretionary cropping. Each coordinate is standardized
by its median and interquartile range (IQR) in a separate development collection
of 221 works from four painters, with equal total weight per development painter.
Neither current references nor generated images fit this transformation.

For reference vectors $X=\{x_i\}$ and generated vectors $Y=\{y_j\}$, let
$a_i,b_j$ be nonnegative weights summing to one within each set. We use the
empirical V-statistic energy discrepancy
\begin{align}
\E(X,Y)={}&2\sum_{i,j}a_i b_j\|x_i-y_j\|_2
-\sum_{i,k}a_i a_k\|x_i-x_k\|_2 \nonumber\\
&-\sum_{j,l}b_j b_l\|y_j-y_l\|_2.
\label{eq:energy}
\end{align}
This statistic incorporates distances within and between the two collections.
Its finite-sample baseline need not be zero for independent samples from the
same population. We interpret lower values as closer empirical distributions
in the specified feature geometry, not as a calibrated degree of artistic
similarity.

References have equal weight, $a_i=1/N_p$. If painter $p$ has $N_{pc}$ reference
works in class $c$ and a generated cell has $n_{pc}$ available images in that
class, a generated image receives $b_j=N_{pc}/(N_p n_{pc})$. Thus both distributions
have the reference panel's class proportions. This weighting reduces differences
in broad content mixture but leaves within-class content differences unresolved.

To measure spread, we use the weighted population-form covariance trace
\begin{equation}
\V(Y)=\sum_j b_j\|y_j-\bar y\|_2^2,
\qquad \bar y=\sum_j b_jy_j.
\label{eq:trace}
\end{equation}
We distinguish the generated/reference ratio $\V(Y)/\V(X)$ from the
named/artist-free ratio $\V(Y_{\mathrm N})/\V(Y_{\mathrm F})$.
A value below one has a different comparison target in these two ratios.
Coordinate-wise squared IQR sums provide a complementary spread summary.

Repeated descriptions allow an exact descriptive decomposition. For description
$b$, let $w_b$ be its total image weight and $\bar y_b$ its weighted mean. Then
\begin{equation}
\V(Y)=
\underbrace{\sum_b\sum_{j\in b}b_j\|y_j-\bar y_b\|_2^2}_{W:\ \text{within descriptions}}
+\underbrace{\sum_b w_b\|\bar y_b-\bar y\|_2^2}_{B:\ \text{between description means}}.
\label{eq:decomposition}
\end{equation}
The between-description term can be further separated into within-class and
between-class components. With three repetitions per description, these are
finite-set summaries, not bias-corrected estimates of population variance
components.
In particular, the between-description term also includes noise in means
estimated from only three images.

\subsection{Prompt contrasts and inference}
The prespecified inferential family comprises six named-minus-artist-free
energy contrasts and two detailed-minus-generic named contrasts on OAuth. A
contrast uses complete pairs sharing the description, repetition and assigned
batch, with the same content weights in both conditions. The paired statistic
retains all terms in Equation~\ref{eq:energy}; it is not a difference of average
distances to a reference mean. Appendix~\ref{app:inference} gives the swap identity.

For each contrast, 99,999 Monte Carlo sign assignments yield a two-sided
randomization $p$-value with the plus-one correction. Holm adjustment controls
the fixed family of eight tests at level .05. The sharp null states that prompt
assignment changes neither availability nor measured features in any assigned
position, under the fixed request and technical-retry policy and no interference.
For a pairwise OAuth comparison, the third condition's position is conditioned
on. The tests concern this finite design and reference panel. They do not test
an oeuvre-wide mean effect, perceptual equivalence or equality of original and
generated population distributions. Treatment-dependent call duration or service
carryover could violate no interference.

The prompt inventory, primary metrics and test family were specified before
current evaluation features were measured. Feature-view, variance-decomposition,
reference-influence, painter-interaction and additional detection/coverage
analyses were designed after the primary results were observed. These subsequent
analyses are descriptive; they introduce no additional significance tests or
population confidence intervals.

\subsection{Measurement and reference diagnostics}
We examine three processing pipelines: the primary 512-pixel pipeline, a
256-pixel version and common JPEG encoding at quality 90 with 4:2:0 subsampling
at 512 pixels. Each uses its own development-only scaler. Four feature views
cross these pipelines: all 31 coordinates; 28 coordinates after dropping three
derived multiscale summaries; 31 coordinates divided by the square root of their
family size; and the 19 color/spatial coordinates with texture removed. The
family-rescaled squared distance sums mean squared differences within each
family; it does not equalize observed family variance or perceptual importance.
This gives 12 views per service--painter comparison.

Reference diagnostics delete one work, holding-collection membership group or
content class at a time. Collection groups use exact recorded sets of holding-
collection IDs and do not identify photographic capture workflows. Work and
collection-group deletions preserve the original content-class masses while
reweighting the remaining works within each class; class deletion renormalizes
the surviving class masses. A separate
analysis refits the scaler after omitting each development
painter. Painter specificity uses equal one-third content-class weights for
both painters. With $G_M,G_C$ denoting collections assigned to Monet and
C\'ezanne, define
\begin{equation}
I=\E(X_M,G_M)+\E(X_C,G_C)-\E(X_M,G_C)-\E(X_C,G_M).
\label{eq:specificity}
\end{equation}
Negative $I$ favors own-painter over cross-painter alignment. The within-reference
and within-generated terms cancel, making this a contrast of relative alignment.
Artist-free prompts are identical across the two collection labels and provide
a descriptive control for their arbitrary painter assignment.

For visualization, we fit one common principal-component analysis (PCA) per
painter, assigning half the weight to references and sharing half equally across
the seven generated cells, with content weighting within each. All cells use
that painter's common axes. Full-space linear and radial-basis-function (RBF)
kernel-ridge classifiers use six folds holding out entire generated descriptions
and disjoint original works, without tuning or PCA inputs. This grouping respects
the repeated-prompt structure \citep{roberts2017}. Cross-service evaluation trains
on one service and tests on another using the same held-out description/work
scheme. We report balanced accuracy at a fixed zero decision threshold and mean
within-fold AUC separately; the former also depends on calibration. Technical
classifier and coverage details appear in Appendix~\ref{app:diagnostics}.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Painter naming improves primary feature proximity}
Painter naming lowers primary energy discrepancy in all six service--painter
comparisons (Figure~\ref{fig:primary}). NB2 decreases from 4.188 to 3.342 for
Monet and from 4.704 to 2.886 for C\'ezanne; FLUX decreases from 1.990 to 1.365
and from 1.700 to 0.804, respectively. These four contrasts reject the conditional
sharp null after adjustment across the eight-test family
(Table~\ref{tab:contrasts}). The OAuth differences are smaller, and neither
survives adjustment. The detailed-versus-generic comparisons likewise provide
no rejection after adjustment. Non-rejection does not establish equivalence.

The same primary feature space also shows that the named collections have less
aggregate spread than the references. Generated/reference trace ratios for the
six detailed named cells range from .354 to .756, whereas the four NB2 and FLUX
artist-free cells range from 1.069 to 1.535. Thus, reduced spread is not a general
property of all generated images in this experiment. It is associated with the
tested painter-naming conditions. The two generic named OAuth cells have still
lower reference-relative ratios, .306 for Monet and .395 for C\'ezanne, although
their energy contrasts do not establish an advantage of detailed prompting.

Energy and spread are distinct summaries but are not statistically independent:
Equation~\ref{eq:energy} includes a within-generated distance term. These results
therefore describe the joint behavior of two measurements, not evidence for a
necessary trade-off between fidelity and diversity.

Under equal content-class weights, naming also strengthens relative
own-painter alignment. The interaction $I$ changes from .101 to $-.855$ for
NB2, from $-.048$ to $-1.755$ for FLUX and from $-.044$ to $-1.802$ for OAuth.
Its named-minus-free change is negative in all 36 service/view/pipeline
combinations. This joint contrast does not require each generated collection
individually to be closest to its own painter's references. Separately generated
artist-free collections have identical
prompt payloads across painter labels yet nonzero empirical discrepancies.
These controls illustrate finite-set
and collection variability; they do not provide an equivalence threshold.

\subsection{Aggregate contraction has different within-prompt components}
The named/artist-free total-trace ratios range from .331 to .697 in the primary
representation. Decomposition reveals different sources of this contraction
(Figure~\ref{fig:variation}). FLUX reduces both within-description and
between-description variation strongly: the respective ratios are .309 and .337
for Monet, and .489 and .451 for C\'ezanne. For NB2/C\'ezanne, within-description
variation decreases to .786 while between-description variation decreases to
.378. Its aggregate reduction therefore conceals a much larger relative change
between descriptions than between repetitions of a description.

The OAuth/C\'ezanne case shows why the distinction is necessary. Total trace
falls to .651 of its artist-free value even though within-description trace
rises to 1.139; between-description trace falls to .583. A tighter overall cloud
can therefore coexist with more variable repetitions. These observations do not
identify a latent model mechanism or demonstrate fewer perceptually distinct
outputs. They locate the measured variation in the actual repeated-prompt design.

\subsection{Contraction is more consistent than proximity across feature views}
All 72 named/artist-free total-trace ratios across the four feature views and
three pipelines are below one, ranging from .308 to .816. Proximity is less
consistent: 66 of 72 energy changes are negative. Both FLUX painter comparisons
and NB2/C\'ezanne remain negative in all 12 view/pipeline combinations. For
NB2/Monet, the 256-pixel color/spatial-only comparison instead increases energy
by .416. The same no-texture view at 512 pixels gives a named/reference trace
ratio of 1.101. Consequently, robust contraction relative to artist-free outputs
does not imply robust below-reference spread or uniformly improved proximity.

The sensitivity has a substantive measurement basis. Texture coordinates
contribute 49.0\% and 47.1\% of primary reference trace for Monet and C\'ezanne.
Some multiscale coordinates are strongly correlated in development data.
Agreement among such coordinates does not constitute independent evidence of the same
artistic property. The diagnostic views probe this dependence without selecting
a preferred representation from the observed effects.

Deleting individual reference works, holding-collection groups or content classes preserves
the direction of all four NB2/FLUX primary proximity changes. These directions
also survive omission of any one development painter from scaling. Some OAuth
changes cross zero under these perturbations. Deletion stability supports the
paid-service comparisons within the available panel, but does not correct shared
reproduction differences or turn that panel into an oeuvre sample.

\subsection{Visible overlap and detectability answer different questions}
The common PCA views show overlapping clouds with changes in location and
concentration (Figure~\ref{fig:pca}). The first two components retain 48.4\% of
weighted variation for Monet and 46.0\% for C\'ezanne, leaving more than half
outside the plots. Visual overlap consequently cannot establish equality of the
full distributions. Conversely, separation in a projection would not establish
a difference in artistic style.

In the full feature space, within-service RBF balanced accuracy ranges from
.893 to .987 across the 14 original/generated comparisons. However, all 576
NB2/FLUX images are square and all 70 references are nonsquare: a rule using
only square geometry separates these domains perfectly. Capture workflows for
the reference reproductions are also unresolved. High classification accuracy
therefore establishes differences between the recorded image domains, not a
validated stylistic distinction.

Cross-service behavior further limits detector interpretation. At the fixed
threshold, naming lowers balanced accuracy in every directed service/painter
comparison for both kernels. Named-condition ranges are .410--.874 for the
linear kernel and .447--.704 for RBF. This need not mean uniformly weaker
ranking discrimination. For Monet with training on FLUX and testing on OAuth,
linear balanced accuracy falls from .904 to .874 while mean within-fold AUC
rises from .953 to .968. The observed threshold transfer includes calibration
changes; below-chance scores are not evidence that generated images have become
more authentic.

\section{Discussion}
The central result is that painter naming changes proximity and variation
together, but the two measurements require separate interpretation. In the
primary representation, NB2 and FLUX generate distributions closer to their
painter references under naming, accompanied by lower total variation. The
repeated-description design shows why describing this simply as ``diversity
loss'' is insufficient. Naming can reduce differences between responses to
different descriptions while leaving variability between repetitions relatively
intact, or even increasing it. Evaluations that pool all generated images into
one cloud conceal this distinction.

One possible explanation is that painter instructions encourage recurrent visual
choices across otherwise different scenes. Our measurements are compatible with
that interpretation but do not distinguish it from changes in depicted content,
composition or rendering. In particular, broad prompt classes do not guarantee
matched realized scenes. Human judgments of set-level stylistic range and
independent content coding would be needed to test whether the observed changes
correspond to stylistic concentration as perceived by viewers.

The representation also determines what ``closer'' means. Interpretable
coordinates make color, spatial and texture contributions inspectable, but they
do not provide a complete or independently validated model of style. Texture
has substantial weight in the current feature geometry, and the NB2/Monet
counterexample prevents a representation-independent proximity claim. The 72
sensitivity comparisons reuse the same images and are not 72 independent
replications. Robustness across their fixed views is evidence about measurement
choices, not about new painters, content inventories or generation runs.

Reference quality constrains the absolute comparison more strongly than image
count alone. The current panel contains selected, unequally represented content
classes and digital reproductions with unresolved capture workflows. Shared
aspect ratio within the paid named/free comparisons removes a simple binary
geometry difference between those prompt arms, but not the original/generated
geometry mismatch or other rendering and content effects. Collection duration,
mutable service implementations and the OAuth quality imbalance further limit
transportability. None of these results supplies a universal model ranking.

The next validation should therefore use independently coded reference panels,
controlled reproductions and human judgments of the same generated sets. A
learned representation could test another feature geometry, but agreement with
it would not by itself establish perceptual validity. Additional generations
are most informative when they replicate the intervention with new descriptions,
painter cases or independently repeated collections, rather than merely enlarge
the current cells.

\section{Conclusion}
For these Monet and C\'ezanne reference panels, painter naming often improves
primary feature proximity while reducing aggregate generated variation. Within the fixed diagnostic grid, aggregate
contraction is more consistent across measurement variants than proximity
improvement, and it does not uniformly reflect reduced variation between
repetitions. Distribution-based evaluation of generated art should therefore
report what changes within and between prompts, alongside its reference and
representation choices. The present results establish a bounded computational
finding, not an assessment of artistic worth or perceptual style reproduction.

\section{Paired randomization statistic}
\label{app:inference}
Let $A_i,B_i$ be a retained pair with common weight $b_i$, and define
$C(v)=\sum_j a_j\|v-x_j\|_2$ and
$T(v)=\sum_i b_i(\|v-A_i\|_2+\|v-B_i\|_2)$. Then
\begin{equation}
 c_i=b_i\{2[C(B_i)-C(A_i)]-[T(B_i)-T(A_i)]\},\qquad
 \sum_i c_i=\E(X,B)-\E(X,A).
\end{equation}
Complementary pair swaps produce $\sum_i s_i c_i$, $s_i\in\{-1,1\}$, exactly,
including the within-generated distances. Independent randomized condition
positions justify the sign assignments under the stated sharp null. Repeated
scene descriptions are not treated as independently sampled scenes for
population inference. With $e$ sign draws at least as extreme in absolute value
as the observed statistic, including ties, $p=(1+e)/100{,}000$. All contrasts
retain 24 distinct descriptions; the two missing generic outputs reduce only
the C\'ezanne detailed/generic comparison to 70 pairs.

\section{Supporting distribution diagnostics}
\label{app:diagnostics}
For $d=31$ standardized coordinates, the fixed kernels are
$K_{\mathrm L}(u,v)=u^\top v/d+1$ and
$K_{\mathrm R}(u,v)=\exp(-\|u-v\|_2^2/d)+1$.
The classifiers minimize weighted squared error with a kernel penalty of .01,
using equal total training weight for original and generated labels and the
reference content proportions within each. Training and evaluation memberships
are fixed. Sparse reference classes mean not every test fold contains all three
classes; pooled balanced accuracy uses the complete held-out set. For cross-service evaluation, AUC is computed within each fold and then
averaged across six folds, rather than pooling margins from differently trained
classifiers. Complete kernels, memberships and scores are included with the
analysis code and numeric tables.

Reference coverage assigns each original a ball whose radius is the distance
to its $k$th nearest distinct original, and measures the reference mass with at
least one generated neighbor inside that ball. We examine $k=1,3,5$ and both
uniform and reference-class-matched generated subsamples, without replacement.
For each setting, 100 draws use either $N_p$ or $\lfloor N_p/2\rfloor$ generated
images; half-size class counts use largest-remainder allocation.
The operating point matters: FLUX/C\'ezanne/named coverage is .438, .750 and
.969 at these three neighborhood sizes with all generated images available.
For OAuth/Monet, the named-minus-free direction at $k=3$ changes from negative
with all generated images to positive for median class-matched subsample coverage.
Thus coverage is not an invariant measure of how much of a painter's style has
been reproduced.

Disjoint reference subsets provide a finite-panel real/real control. Each draw
allocates $\lfloor N_{pc}/2\rfloor$ works per class to each subset, giving 18
Monet or 15 C\'ezanne works per side; these draws reuse the same panel. At $k=3$,
median coverage of reduced reference anchors is .944 and .933 for held-out real
queries, compared with .833 and .800 for FLUX named queries at matched sizes.
At $k=5$, both real controls and the C\'ezanne FLUX named control reach median
coverage one. Such saturation does not establish distributional equivalence,
and repeated subsets do not supply independent reference replications.
